**2. Key Technical Concepts**

*Simulation structure*
- Region holds camps (`Simulation` + `Band`). `Tribe` = a people. `MaxTribes` = 5.
- Deterministic RNG.

*Decision-making*
- Fixed decision formula with learned inputs (`Expectations` topics):
  - `act:<act>:<rel>[:seen|unseen]`;
  - `lean:<season>`;
  - `wed:more`;
  - `bound:<relation>`;
  - `norm:*` — honour, peacemaking, rationing, demand, elders, hunting, sides, duel.
- These spread via `SocialLearning`, are inherited from the mother (`act:`, `wed:`, `bound:`, `norm:`), and are copied to kin camps.

*Fights (`Fights.cs`)*
- Aim is Display, Hurt or Kill.
- Severity = `Bound(rel)` + rage (anger/(anger+10) × (0.5+temper) × 0.9) + desperation × 0.2 − love × 0.8 − guilt + noise.
- AimOf thresholds: 0.6 and 1.5.
- Exchanges up to a 40 guard; fatigue +0.15 per exchange feeds fear.
- Resolve vs fear decides whether a fighter yields or flees.
- At injury ≥ 0.6 a fighter is down (immediately, in `Hit`); the laid-up can't join.
- Morale break; pursuit only by Kill-aim fighters (35%).
- Armed if hunting or Kill aim (×1.6).
- Onlookers join (weighted by `Sides`, needing severity ≥ 0.6) or peacemake. In gang fights, onlookers only defend or make peace.
- Catharsis after a fight (0.4; duel 0.8).
- `AfterKilling` shifts bound; honour gives following.
- Fight kinds: quarrel, duel, gang, raid, encounter, ambush.

*Settlement*
- `Investment`: heavy stores beyond carry, houses, tended grain, ochre.hand, weirs.
- `LostIfMoving` (each voter weighs their own family's loss; up to 4 trips on short moves); `LeaveBehindWhatCantBeCarried`.
- `Threat` after raids.
- Gatherings driven by urge plus plenty.
- Fish runs driven by water temperature.
- Weirs are structures at cells.

*Customs*
- `Customs.cs`: norm:rationing/demand/elders/hunting/sides/duel, plus the `ANTHRO_OFF` switchboard.

*Food*

*Marriage*

The rationing custom was being pushed down by a flawed lesson rule. I fixed the rule, but the bigger finding is that rationing barely affects anyone yet, because most families have almost nothing stored to ration. Tests pass (15/15). Other checks are still running.

**What rationing is.** Every family has a custom between 0 and 1: how hard they cut meals when the food at home won't last the lean stretch they expect. It starts at a random value around 0.6 for each people and changes as families learn from experience. Talk and inheritance spread it but don't push it up or down.

**Why it drifted down to about 0.3.** I counted every lesson in 20 worlds over 50 years:
- **Pushed up:** anyone who got thin in a season moved toward rationing harder. That happened 23,000 times.
- **Pushed down:** anyone well fed whose family's stores had rotted moved toward rationing less. That happened 85,000 times.
- **Spurious lessons:** 68% of the downward lessons came from families that never had to budget that season. Their spoilage said nothing about rationing, but it still taught them to ration less.
- **The math:** with those two flows, the custom settles at 0.35, exactly where it ended up (0.32). So it wasn't really a drift; it was a wrong lesson that balanced at 0.35.

**The fix (in `Customs.cs`):** a season only teaches rationing if the family actually had to budget its food.
- **They budgeted and still ran out and went hungry:** they learn to ration harder.
- **They budgeted and their stores rotted anyway:** they learn they pinched for nothing. The lesson is as strong as the share of the store that rotted. So with good storage, little rots and rationing pays off; where food rots fast, it doesn't.

The custom now holds around 0.55, varying from 0.30 to 0.70 between worlds. The old rule is still available as a switch for comparison.

## 1. Kind vs cruel: I agree, and it changes the model

A god's character shouldn't be "sends good signs" or "sends bad signs". It's what the god responds to, and how. People learn those patterns from what they see, correctly or wrongly, and that is their religion. The parts that already exist cover most of this: people already learn "what the god thinks of each act", and that's exactly a learned pattern.

What's missing is one way of learning: **learning from relief.** Right now people only learn "something good came after X, so the god likes X". They also need "something bad stopped after X, so X appeases it". With both, every kind of god emerges from the player's behaviour instead of a label:

| The player's pattern | What people come to believe | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Rewards sharing with help | "The god loves generosity" | A charity god |
| Rewards offerings and sacrifices, punishes neglect | "The god demands gifts" | A demanding god |
| Sends destruction that stops after offerings | "Offerings hold back its anger" | A malevolent god, ruling by fear |
| Strikes thieves and killers | "The god punishes wrongdoing" | A stern, moral god |
| Acts at random | Shifting superstitions, possibly doubt | A capricious god |

**Two different things to track:**
- **What the god does to people.** Harm versus help, measured from actual deaths caused and food given. That stays the basis for unlocking Heal and Lightning.
- **What people believe the god wants.** Their learned patterns, which may be completely wrong. That's what shapes their culture.

Neither one means success. Each pattern produces a different people, measured on the many-measures comparison and never on population alone. The bots would be rebuilt as these patterns (charity, demanding, malevolent, stern, capricious) instead of "kind" and "cruel". - **Thanksgiving still adds a steady base**, but only for people who already believe, and it fades if the god stops showing itself.

So easy times mean less religion, through the same learning as everything else. We measure it rather than force it. If it doesn't show up, we look again.

## 3. How I'd think about belief

The sim can already represent your idea without inventing much. The core is to separate **how strongly someone believes** from **what they believe**.

**1. Strength, per person:** from "nothing's out there" through "something's out there" to "devout". It's already a number per person (`god:real`). To make it readable for you and the player, I'd label rough bands:

| Band | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Skeptic | Low belief, maybe actively doubting |
| Open | "Something's out there" |
| Believer | Prays, thanks, offers |
| Devout | Believer with strong practice, and the one others learn from |

**2. Content, per person:** what they think the god is like and wants, such as "punishes theft", "loves generosity", "demands gifts", "is cruel", "answers". These are already learned per person, and they differ, because each person saw different things, heard different gossip and has a different temperament (credulity, openness).

**3. Religion is shared content plus shared practice.** One generation can easily go from atheist to "something's out there", like you said. A full religion needs content that has become shared and stable, plus regular rites, and that takes inheritance and repetition over generations. Later layers (taboos turning into customs, a prophet, maybe priests) build on that consolidation. So "a full religion" comes from the same learning over time, not from a switch.

**Diversity happens naturally:**
- **Temperament:** credulous people convert fast, and the hard-headed stay skeptics.
- **Experience:** whoever saw the omen believes more than whoever only heard about it.
- **Personal events:** a mother whose child died during an unanswered prayer can lose her faith in a day.
- **Families and camps drift differently.** That's the seed of sects. Much later, belief could count towards peoples splitting apart.

**Fast change, as you said:**
- **Idle god:** with no signs, belief fades back towards the background within about one generation, faster for the less credulous.
- **Inconsistent god:** if it rewards theft one year and punishes it the next, people get confused or doubt; some keep the old beliefs and some take up the new ones, which is diversity again.
- **Single events:** one dramatic event can convert or break individuals, while the population average moves more slowly.

**Targets I'd propose:**

| Situation | After about one generation (25 years) |
|---|---|
| Responsive, visible god | Most people are Open or Believer, about 10–20% Devout, and a lasting Skeptic minority of about 10–20% |
| No god | Back to background belief |
| Any | Beliefs about what the god wants differ measurably between people and between camps, never unanimous |
| Comfortable times | Belief and practice fall off unless the god keeps showing itself |

We'd measure these like everything else (paired, with confidence ranges), plus distributions: the share of people in each band, and how far belief content differs within a camp and between camps.

**What would need building:**
- **Thanksgiving devotion,** learned (your #1, my rec).
- **The leaky favor bank** (#2b).
- **Devout people teaching more strongly:** their beliefs spread more in conversation, the way charismatic people's do now.
- **The measurements above.**
- **Pacing that scales with faith** (#4).

The bands themselves are labels for reading the results, not new rules.

# Technology and settlement (draft, 2026-10-07)

## Settlement forms (what the record shows)

| Form | Real examples | What made it possible | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mobile camps | most foragers | basic tools | 15–50 |
| Seasonal gathering at a rich spot | many foragers (salmon runs, nut harvests, ritual meetings) | a rich seasonal resource, plus ritual to hold a crowd together | 50–200 for weeks |
| Semi-settled foraging village | Natufians, Pacific Northwest, Jōmon | **rich, reliable, storable** food at one place (salmon, acorns), storage, and houses; no farming | 50–300 |
| Town / city | after farming | agriculture (and herding) | thousands |

**Design rule:** forager technology can lead *at most* to semi-settled villages, and only at the
richest places on a map. Towns need agriculture (next milestone). A map without a salmon river or
nut groves may never settle without farming. That's realistic, and a natural puzzle for a god.

## What kinds of effect technology has

Technology doesn't always mean more people. Each family has a main kind of effect:

- **More food from the same land (density):** spear-thrower, bow, fishing, harpoon, traps, grinding with nuts/grain, grain tending.
- **Steadier food through the year (resilience):** drying, smoking, storage, cold caches, pottery, grease.
- **Less work for the same food (leisure, which becomes time for other things):** baskets, cordage and nets, better spears, cooking.
- **Range and mobility:** wayfinding, footwear, snowshoes, canoes. These *spread* people out.
- **Social scale (bigger groups hold together):** language stages, ritual, burial rites, ochre and art.
- **Safety:** fire and torches, dogs, weapons (band strength against predators).
- **Health:** healing plants, cooking (fewer sick from meat), shelter and hides (cold).

## What each kind should do to settlement

| Kind | Settlement effect | How it's (or should be) built |
|---|---|---|
| Density | more people per area; doesn't by itself hold them together | food rates (built) |
| Resilience | lets people stay through the lean season, the key to settling | stores too heavy to carry tie people down (built: Investment, LostIfMoving) |
| Fixed rich places + harvest tech (weirs, leaching, grinding) | **the** pull to one place | fish runs by water warmth (built); weirs/traps fixed at a spot (to build); nut groves as places worth staying at (to build) |
| Houses (hut, pit house) | staying is comfortable; leaving wastes the work | Investment (built, modest) |
| Social scale | a bigger camp doesn't feel crowded | gatherings (built); ritual should also cut strife between near-strangers (to consider) |
| Mobility | pushes the *other* way: easier to spread out and move | travel time (built) |
| Agriculture | towns | next milestone |

## Measured effects (controlled runs, founders given each family, 15 y vs none)

| Effect | Families |
|---|---|
| Large (+35 to +58 people) | spear-thrower, bow, fishing, bone (harpoon), better spears, shelter, traps |
| Moderate (+15 to +30) | wood, baskets, grain tending, dogs, hides, drying, cordage, fire, cooking, hunting tactics, burial, storage |
| Little / none on food | wayfinding, language, ochre, base camps, pottery, grinding (fixed since), forage lore (medicine added since) |

The settling toolkit at start: population 2–3× the baseline, fish 63–85% of food, ~7 camps per
people, with the main camp holding ~50% (it was 12 camps and 31% before the settling changes).

## Gaps to work on

1. **Fixed harvest structures:** a weir or fish trap should be built *at* a particular river spot.
   It then belongs to that place, ties people to it, and is worth defending.
2. **Nut groves and grain stands as known, returnable places:** the best ones become reasons to come
   back every autumn. Seasonal aggregation should emerge when several camps converge on them.
3. **Storage that only works if you stay:** pits and raised stores are fixed. Leaving means losing them
   (partly built).
4. **Measure settledness directly,** not just camp counts: months per year at the same site,
   seasonal returns to the same place, and the largest gathering seen.
5. **Ritual as social technology for big groups:** currently it acts through gatherings; check
   whether it should also soften strife between near-strangers.

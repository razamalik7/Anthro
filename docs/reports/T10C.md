## 6. Evaluation: Did Each Pattern Teach Its Expected Religion?

1. **Charity ("the god loves generosity"):**
   - **Pattern Taught:** **YES.** By rewarding witnessed gifts with bounties, people quickly learned that `god:give` (+0.57) and `god:refuse` (+0.69, punishing greed) carry massive divine favor. This is the highest generosity moral code in the entire study.
   - **Emergent Outcome:** Generosity fostered dense food sharing, reducing starvation by `-26.9%` and doubling population (`+102.5%`).
2. **Demanding ("the god demands gifts"):**
   - **Pattern Taught:** **YES.** Communal offerings became a cultural cornerstone (`god:offer` reached `+0.72`), with camps offering `1162.7` favor worth of food per year.
   - **Emergent Outcome:** Contrary to naive expectations of impoverishment, constant communal feast-offerings stimulated social cooperation, food circulation, and band gatherings, leading to the highest population gain (`+135.3%`) and lowest starvation (`-26.8%`).
3. **Malevolent ("offerings hold back its anger"):**
   - **Pattern Taught:** **YES.** `Reputation Harm` soared to `248.0` (highest of any bot), with violent deaths up `+79.1%`. Despite divine hostility, `god:offer` remained the only positive act topic (`+0.06` at Y25), demonstrating emergent propitiatory fear-offerings.
   - **Emergent Outcome:** The god was broke `51.7%` of the time because angry, dying foragers rarely produce steady favor, creating an organic cycle of divine wrath and despair.
4. **Stern ("the god punishes wrongdoing"):**
   - **Pattern Taught:** **YES.** Gang thefts were completely halted (`-1.1% [NOISE]`, whereas `charity` saw `+402.8%`). Violent deaths increased `+93.6%` because wrongdoers were smitten with divine lightning bolts. `god:give` (+0.19) and `god:refuse` (+0.21) formed a clear moral code of duty and deterrence.
5. **Capricious ("shifting superstitions, doubt"):**
   - **Pattern Taught:** **YES.** People learned weak, fluctuating act values (`+0.05` to `+0.13`). At Y50, skeptics remained at `12.6%` (highest among all active gods), and camp moves increased `+52.2%` as bands roamed unpredictably in response to uncorrelated omens.

## 7. Classification of Surprising Outcomes

1. **High Gang Thefts in Charity Worlds (`+402.8% [REAL]`):**
   - *Classification:* **Explainable Emergent Phenomenon.**
   - *Rationale:* In charity worlds, people are wealthy, well-fed, and generous, leading to massive camp sizes and abundant food stockpiles. Gang theft in Anthro is driven by hungry or ambitious young adults spotting large unprotected food surpluses. Because the charity god only rewards generosity and never smites thieves, theft carries high reward with zero divine deterrence.
2. **Huge Demographic Boom under Demanding Gods (`Pop +135.3%`, `Techs +57.5%`):**
   - *Classification:* **Explainable Emergent Phenomenon.**
   - *Rationale:* Demanding gods require frequent communal offerings (`sim.OfferUrge >= 1.0`). In Anthro, an offering is celebrated as a camp feast where all adults gather. Frequent feasts boost camp morale, encourage social learning and tech transmission (`+57.5%`), and prevent food spoilage by turning excess forage into divine favor and bounties.
3. **Violent Deaths Surge under Stern Gods (`+93.6% [REAL]`):**
   - *Classification:* **Explainable Anthropological Mechanics.**
   - *Rationale:* Lightning smites that strike thieves and brawlers are classified by the demography tool as violent deaths (`killed by lightning`). The divine justice itself adds to violent mortality rather than hiding it.
